# Two-way door

**Source:** Jeff Bezos. Amazon shareholder letters: 2015 (Type 1 and Type 2 decisions) and 2016 (decide with about 70% of the information you wish you had; disagree and commit). Downside capping from Richard Branson, "Losing My Virginity" (1998): he got Boeing to take the Virgin Atlantic 747 back if the airline failed in year one. Protect the downside, then chase the upside.

**Use when:** every decision. This runs first and sets the speed.

## The questions, in order

1. Can we walk back through this door cheaply? Count the reversal cost in time, money, and trust.
2. **Two-way door.** Decide now with about 70% of the info. Act. Fix it later if wrong. Do not escalate.
3. **One-way door.** Slow down. What is the worst case? Can we survive it? Can we cap it with a small test, an exit clause, or a return option? Then get more input, and escalate if an `AGENTS.md` gate applies.
4. Waiting past 70% is its own cost. Name what the delay costs.

## Output

- Door: two-way / one-way
- Reversal cost:
- Worst case + cap (one-way only):
- Speed: decide now / slow down / escalate

## Traps

- Treating every decision as one-way. Most are two-way.
- Treating a one-way door as two-way because it feels small. Money, sends, deletes, and production changes are one-way by default.
