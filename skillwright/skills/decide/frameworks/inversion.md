# Inversion, with a pre-mortem

**Source:** Charlie Munger, "Invert, always invert," borrowed from mathematician Carl Jacobi. His 1986 Harvard-Westlake commencement speech and "Poor Charlie's Almanack." Pre-mortem: Gary Klein, Harvard Business Review, 2007. Daniel Kahneman calls it his favorite fix for overconfidence in "Thinking, Fast and Slow" (2011).

**Use when:** a plan is about to be committed, or you are stuck between options.

## The questions, in order

1. What would guarantee failure here? List the ways. Be specific.
2. Which of those are we doing right now? Stop those first.
3. **Pre-mortem.** It is 12 months from now. This failed badly. Why? Each person writes their reasons alone before anyone talks.
4. What is the top cause? What do we change today to remove it?
5. Now pick. If the choice is still open, run hell yes or no.

## Output

- Guaranteed-failure list:
- Doing now, stopped:
- Top pre-mortem cause + change:
- Pick:

## Traps

- Listing only outside causes. Include our own behavior.
- Doing the pre-mortem out loud first. The loudest voice sets the list.
